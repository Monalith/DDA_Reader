## 2. alignRef: the "two peaks, one kerb" merge rule

**The rule.** Same-direction |κ| peaks ≤ 60 m apart are treated as one corner when the smoothed |κ|
between them dips by less than 30 % of the smaller peak. A merged group serves at most one reference
corner, and the apex goes to the member nearest the expected position.

**The code.** In `src/sim/alignRef.ts`:

- `MERGE_GAP_M` and `MERGE_DIP_FRAC` hold the two thresholds.
- `peakGroups()` builds the groups.
- `alignReference(…, { mergePeaks })` turns the rule on per call.
- `alignDefaults.mergePeaks` is the default, and it is **off**.

**Does the rule trigger for K11/K12 on the pist line?** Not for the pair the reread note meant. The
pist's K11-apex-2 (2071) and K12 (2116) appear on the lines as follows:

| line | the suspected pair | gap | dip | merged? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pist centreline | 2058 / 2127 m | 69 m | 41 % | no |
| race κ 0 kerb 1 (working-tree line) | 2087 / 2154 m | 67 m | 23 % | no (gap) |
| race κ 0 kerb 0 | 2081 / 2147 m | 66 m | 23 % | no (gap) |

**What it does trigger on: K11's own two right peaks.** These are K11's apex and the kerb-side peak.

| line | K11's peaks | gap | dip |
|---|---|---|---|
| centreline | 2002 / 2058 | 56 m | 7 % |
| κ 0 kerb 0 | 2022 / 2081 | 60 m | 8 % |
| κ 0 kerb 1, HEAD line | 2037 / 2095 | 58 m | — |
| κ 0.85 lines | 1902 / 1950 and 1914 / 1963 | — | ~5 % |

Merging them takes the kerb-side peak away from K12, which then falls back to the exit peak. That is the
exact error v2 removes. Also merged, but harmless: K9's run of peaks, K2, K13 and K15 on some lines.

- **On v1** the rule changes only the map line's K11 (1959 → 1877).
- **Match count.** It stays **16 ↔ 16** on every line, with the rule on or off.
- **Unmatched corners.** None is added. The only unmatched-corner note is the existing "K14: no left
  peak", on the map line and on HEAD's κ 0 kerb 0.

**Decision: the rule is opt-in and off by default.** With the rule off, v2 already puts K12 on the
kerb-side peak on every pist line, because its apex is now at the kerb. The rule would undo that on
5 of 6 lines. Its 60 m threshold also sits on a knife edge at K11: the gap is 58–63 m depending on the
line build. Tests pin both behaviours (§4).

### Reading

- **K8 (v2 112 km/h) drops by 0.26–0.30 g on every line.**
  - κ 0: **1.23 g** (kerb 1.0) and **1.33 g** (kerb 0), both under the ceiling.
  - κ 0.85: still 1.42 g (kerb 1.0) and 1.49 g (kerb 0). That line is wrong elsewhere too (K1–K4, K9,
    K11, K16 are all over 1.47 g).
  - These match the reread note's estimate of 1.25 / 1.35 g.
- **K12 at the apex drops to 1.42 / 1.43 g on κ 0.** The note estimated 1.23 g, by scaling v1's apex
  radius. v2's apex is the kerb-side |κ| peak, which is tighter: R 65 m against R 78 m at v1's exit
  peak. 106 km/h on R 65 m is 1.42 g.
- **K12's zone peak is new and large: 2.10 / 2.14 g on κ 0.**
  - **Where.** Sim 2150–2175 m, R 78–83 m: the same peak v1 called the apex.
  - **Why so high.** The reference is at 138–143 km/h there (t 57.2–57.4 s), against v1's 124 km/h,
    because that point is no longer pinned to the apex speed.
  - **What the video shows there.** From 56.3 s the kerb has ended and the bike runs out to the
    centre/outside, still leaning. So the real path at 2150–2175 m has a much larger radius than the
    κ 0 line's second peak.
  - **Conclusion.** This is the line-model artefact of reread note §6.5: the race line puts a second
    apex where the bike takes one kerb and runs wide. A ±20 m reference shift does not help.
- **The apex-speed fit improves.** Mean |Δ| drops to 0.03–0.08 km/h, and two lines now converge.
- **The lap moves a little against the reference speed ridden on the same line.**
  - κ 0: +0.12 s.
  - κ 0.85 kerb 1.0: −0.16 s.
  - Most of this is K12: the sim cannot follow the reference's 138–143 km/h through a 2 g zone peak, so
    it loses time there.
- **K2, K4 and K6 (κ 0) do not move.**
  - **K2 and K4** are identical on v1 and v2.
  - **K6** moves by +0.003 g only. It is inside the re-scaled K5→K8 span, but its apex is fixed.
  - None of these is a v2 data issue.
